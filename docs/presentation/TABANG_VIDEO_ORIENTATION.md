# TABANG — Video Orientation Guide & Script

**Format:** screen-recorded product orientation with voiceover  
**Length:** 7:30–8:00 for the full master; 2:30–3:00 for the short cut  
**Audience:** common viewers — donors, patients and families, chapter staff, and panelists who need to understand TABANG without a technical lecture  
**Narrators:** three presenters, one per chapter  
**Depth:** a guided tour of the blood workflow, not a button-by-button manual  
**Tone:** plain, calm, friendly; use natural transitions and contractions, not a list of labels

---

## 1. Video specification

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Aspect ratio | 16:9 |
| Resolution | 1920 × 1080 |
| Frame rate | 30 fps |
| Audio | One clean voice track; light background music at −24 dB under the voice |
| Captions | Burned-in or uploaded `.srt`; include the demo account labels |
| Recording | Screen recording of the web build, browser at 100–110% zoom |
| Cursor | Visible, slow, deliberate movements; no rapid circling |
| Notifications | Off; close chat/email before recording |
| Data | Demo accounts only; no real patient information |
| Privacy | Never show real names, emails, phone numbers, or uploaded document content |

### Language rules

TABANG is for ordinary donors and families, not software reviewers. Use everyday words.

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| blood appeals | posts asking for blood |
| hard to verify / legitimate | hard to check if it is real / real |
| manual processing | paperwork by hand |
| non-remunerated | unpaid |
| sensitive personal data | private health information |
| fulfilled | completed |
| audit record | record of who changed what |
| backend / projections | secure online database / safe public views |

- Explain a term the first time it appears. For example: “blood component, such as red cells or plasma.”
- Use natural transitions such as “Next,” “Then,” and “From there” to connect one screen to the next.
- Contractions are fine — “it's,” “doesn't,” “you'll” — because this is a spoken script, not a formal report.
- Do not read every label or dropdown option.
- Mention support screens once; do not demonstrate them.

### Legal and SDG labels

- **RA 7719 — National Blood Services Act of 1994.** Says blood donation must be voluntary and unpaid, and sets the rules for blood services.
- **RA 10173 — Data Privacy Act of 2012.** Says personal and health information must be kept private and used only for a clear purpose.
- **SDGs — visual labels only.** SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-being) is the clearest fit. Add SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) or SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals) only if the paper explicitly names them. Do not say TABANG “achieves” an SDG in the narration; show the label and let the paper make the argument.

RA 7719 and RA 10173 are the two laws the app already references. If the paper cites another law, add it in the same on-screen style and keep the explanation to one sentence.

### What to show and what to mention

- **Cover each role once, from start to finish.** Community/recipient, donor, staff, and admin.
- **Community user and recipient share one path.** Both can browse blood stock and requests; the recipient owns the request-posting flow. Do not repeat the same screens under two role names.
- **Cover each major tab once.** Show only the buttons that change something or hand work to another role — for example: post request, check request, hold blood, respond, choose a donor, complete, approve, warn, resolve.
- **Treat filters as one idea.** Say what can be filtered, show one example, and move on. Do not open every dropdown.
- **Go deeper on privacy and safety:** a new request is private until staff check it; documents and clinical notes stay with the requester and staff; blood holds and release remain staff decisions.
- **Mention support screens once; do not demonstrate them.** Sign-in, registration, Settings/Profile, About & Help, notifications, and theme/accessibility controls are supporting screens. A single sentence is enough.
- **Leave detailed instructions to the README and Help page.** The video is for understanding; the written guide is for reference.

### Recommended package

| Deliverable | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Master orientation** (one file) | 7:30–8:00 | Paper submission, panel, general audience |
| **Short cut** | 2:30–3:00 | Time-limited defense or a shorter class presentation |
| **Optional role clips** | 2:00–4:00 each | Actual onboarding, only if the chapter will use TABANG separately |

For the paper, submit **one master video**. It preserves the handoff between roles and keeps the privacy story intact. Separate role videos are a training-library idea, not a requirement; if you make them, treat them as extras derived from the master, not replacements. Give each clip the same 10-second intro, simulated-data disclaimer, and end card so the set still feels like one product.

> **Runtime note:** if the paper or panel states a hard limit, that limit wins. For a five-minute video, trim the master to 4:30–5:00. For a three-minute cap, or a five-minute defense slot that also needs Q&A, use the 2:30–3:00 cut. Keep the full version as the reference. Do not speed up the narration to squeeze in more features — cut whole sections instead.

### Three-narrator plan

| Narrator | Chapters | Total |
|---|---|---|
| **Narrator 1 — The need** | Opening, problem, Home, Blood Bank, Community map, posting a request, privacy | ~3:15 |
| **Narrator 2 — The donor and the conversation** | What a donor sees, messaging, closing | ~2:00 |
| **Narrator 3 — The chapter** | Staff operations, drives/accounts/reports, behind the scenes | ~2:45 |

Rules:

- One narrator owns a whole chapter. Do not trade sentences inside a section.
- Record each narrator's chapter separately against the same screen recording; do not record all three live in one take.
- Record a shared five-second handoff ("To show how the chapter handles that, here is ...") so transitions feel intentional.
- Keep the same microphone, room tone, and speaking pace. Normalise loudness across all three voices.
- If one presenter is less comfortable, give them the shorter chapter and let them rehearse the screen actions first.
- Do not record sign-in or setup. Start after the demo account is ready.

### Coverage check

Use this as a planning table, not as narration. Show each role's core path once; the right column can be mentioned in a sentence without opening every control.

| Role | Show on screen | Mention only |
|---|---|---|
| Community / recipient | Home feed (stories, drives, preparation guides), quick buttons, blood stock, Community map (Donors, Facilities, Drives, Requests), request form (documents and time slot), request status, privacy notice, guide button | Holds, profile settings, Help articles |
| Donor | Donor Hub (can I donate?), drives, request board and filters, response, one active request | Donation history, badges |
| Staff | Overview, map and request list, blood stock and holds, request check, booked slots, completion | Reports, change history |
| Admin | Drive approval | Roles, preparedness goals, report oversight |
| Support screens | Nothing | Sign-in, registration, Settings/Profile, About & Help, notifications |

If a role is missing from the master, add a short screen beat or one sentence rather than a new video. The point is to show that every role shares one record and one privacy rule.

### Before recording

1. Run the web build or desktop build.
2. Sign in as the demo account, then start recording after sign-in.
3. Confirm the demo data is reset to its seed state.
4. Set the browser to a clean window with no personal tabs.
5. Prepare the staff and admin tabs in separate windows for quick switching.
6. Have the narration script open on a second screen or printed.
7. Record a 10-second test for audio level and cursor visibility.

### During recording

- Pause for one beat after each screen change.
- Say the **purpose** of a screen before listing its controls.
- Do not read every label; point to the three or four that matter.
- Keep one idea per sentence.
- When showing a private document, stop at the frame and explain the rule; do not open the file.
- Do not show sign-in, Settings, About & Help, or notifications; mention them in the closing.

### After recording

- Trim dead air and sign-in typing.
- Add lower-third labels for each section.
- Add a subtle highlight around the control being discussed.
- Caption the demo accounts and the "simulated data" disclaimer.
- Watch once with sound off to check that the visuals still tell the story.

## 2. Storyboard and narration script

### 0:00–0:30 — Opening · Narrator 1

**Visual:** Clean TABANG title card; slow push-in on the logo.  
**On-screen text:** TABANG — Transfusion Access, Blood Availability and Network Guidance.

**Narration:**

> TABANG is a blood-donation coordination system for Digos City. It connects people who need blood, people who can donate, and the chapter staff who check and manage the process.
>
> This is a research prototype. It uses made-up records, not real patient data, and it isn't connected to a live hospital or Philippine Red Cross system.

### 0:30–1:10 — The problem and the idea · Narrator 1

**Visual:** Title card with three short labels — “Online posts can be fake,” “Paperwork by hand,” “No clear instructions” — and a small “SDG 3” badge; then cut to the TABANG Home screen.  
**On-screen text:** Fake posts · Paper records · No clear instructions · RA 7719 · RA 10173  
**Optional SDG label:** SDG 3 — Good Health and Well-being (visual only; add SDG 10 or 17 only if the paper names them).

**Narration:**

> Many people now ask for blood through social media. These posts can reach a lot of people, but it's hard to check if they are real, and they can reveal private health information.
>
> At the chapter, staff still process requests, papers, and blood stock by hand. Records can be slow to check and easy to lose. Donors and families also need one clear guide to the requirements, the steps, and what to do next.
>
> So TABANG helps with three things: it checks requests before they are shared, it keeps private information private, and it gives each person clear next steps. It follows two Philippine laws: RA 7719 keeps donation voluntary and unpaid, and RA 10173 keeps health information private.

### 1:10–2:15 — Home, Blood Bank, and the Community map · Narrator 1

**Visual:** Home feed — featured story, upcoming drives, announcements and stories, preparation guides — then the quick-action buttons → Blood Bank (chapter banner, stock summary, unit list) → Community map (Donors, Facilities, Drives, and Requests tabs; heat layer and pins).  
**On-screen text:** Home feed · Chapter stock · Request a unit · Community map.

**Narration:**

> Home is the community feed. It starts with a featured story, then shortcuts for requests, donors on the map, or drives. Below that, you'll find upcoming drives, announcements, donor stories, and preparation guides.
>
> Next is Blood Bank. It shows the Davao del Sur chapter's own stock in Digos City. You can browse by blood type, like A+ or O−, and by component, like red cells or plasma. You can request a unit from here, but staff still check the documents and compatibility before release. A request or hold doesn't mean the blood is ready to use.
>
> Then there's the Community tab. It opens the Digos City barangay map with a list beside it. That list has four tabs: donors, facilities, drives, and open requests. On the map, pins mark hospitals, the Philippine Red Cross chapter, and drives. The map can shade each barangay by donor availability or by how many requests are open, so you can see where donors are concentrated or where more people are asking for blood. Tap a barangay to filter the list, or a blood type to narrow the donors. Donor cards show handles like “Donor #001,” not real names.

### 2:15–2:45 — What a donor sees · Narrator 2

**Visual:** Requests board filtered to matching requests; open one card → Donor Hub.  
**On-screen text:** Only checked requests · Match your blood type.

**Narration:**

> For donors, the Requests board only shows requests that chapter staff have checked. A new request stays private until staff review it.
>
> A donor sees requests that match their blood type and the blood component they can give. You can filter by urgency and status to show only the requests you want to see. Before responding, the Donor Hub shows whether they can donate now and when their next donation is allowed. Donation is voluntary and unpaid. This board is for matching, not a public feed of every request.

### 2:45–3:45 — Posting a request and keeping it private · Narrator 1

**Visual:** Requests → Request blood form; documents and time slot; submitted card; privacy notice on another user’s request; filter guide.  
**On-screen text:** Documents required · Chapter checks · Private files.

**Narration:**

> A request is more than a text post. The requester uploads the doctor's request form, the transfusion slip, and the patient ID, then books a time at the blood bank.
>
> The request starts as “Awaiting verification,” which means it's waiting for staff to check it. Staff review the files and confirm the time before other people can see the request.
>
> The medical documents, the clinical note, and the staff review note are visible only to the requester and chapter staff. Another donor can see the hospital, barangay, and urgency, but can't open someone else's files.
>
> The guide button beside the filters explains these steps in plain language, so help is available where you need it.

### 3:45–4:50 — The chapter staff view · Narrator 3

**Visual:** Staff console → Overview → Operations map → Inventory → request check → booked slots.  
**On-screen text:** Check requests · Hold blood · Record the donation.

**Narration:**

> On the chapter side, staff start on the console. Overview shows how prepared the chapter is, what blood is available, and which tasks need attention.
>
> From there, Operations brings the map, blood stock, and drives together. On the map, staff see the request list, the donor list, and where requests are concentrated by barangay.
>
> In Inventory, the blood stock screen, staff add blood units, track expiry, place or release holds, and work through requests. When staff check a request, they review the uploaded files and the booked time; for a blood-bank request, they can hold matching units at that moment.
>
> Booked times and removed units stay in their own lists, so nothing disappears. When the donation is done, staff mark the request completed. Every stock change is recorded and can be undone when appropriate.

### 4:50–5:40 — Drives, accounts, and reports · Narrator 3

**Visual:** Drives tab → Add drive → admin approval → Users → warnings and checks → reports.  
**On-screen text:** Staff propose · Admin approves · Every change is recorded.

**Narration:**

> From the same console, staff can propose a blood drive, but only an admin can approve, reject, or cancel it. Once approved, the drive appears on the donor Home page, the drive list, and the map.
>
> The Users page lets staff check blood cards, give warnings, pause or restore accounts, and record donations. An account can receive up to three warnings; restoring it clears the active count.
>
> When a message or a user is reported, it goes to a review list. Staff can clear a flag, turn it into a warning, mute a conversation, or close a report with the reporter's explanation attached.
>
> Admin-only controls include account roles, chapter targets, and the record of who changed what.

### 5:40–6:30 — Messaging and privacy · Narrator 2

**Visual:** Messages → open a conversation → share-names prompt → report and block controls.  
**On-screen text:** Talk first · Share names by agreement · One active request.

**Narration:**

> Back on the community side, people coordinate in Messages. They start with nicknames. Real names and contact details appear only after both sides agree to share them.
>
> A requester can choose a donor only after they have exchanged a real message. This keeps the choice personal and avoids picking a name from a list with no conversation.
>
> A donor who is already helping with one request is marked “Coordinating” and can't be chosen for another at the same time. Reporting and blocking are in the same conversation, and a block stops messages both ways.

### 6:30–7:20 — What happens behind the scenes · Narrator 3

**Visual:** Simple layered graphic: the app → a secure online database (planned).  
**On-screen text:** One shared record · Each role sees what it needs · Live updates.

**Narration:**

> Behind the screens, this build keeps its data only in memory, so the demo resets when the app restarts. The next phase moves the same records to a secure online database.
>
> That database will use access rules so each role sees only what it needs, safe step-by-step actions for things like checking a request or holding a unit, and live updates so two devices see the same change.
>
> The same privacy rule carries over: public screens see only what is safe, while staff see the full record. That keeps the app simple without weakening privacy.

### 7:20–8:00 — Closing · Narrator 2

**Visual:** Return to the Home screen or a clean title card.  
**On-screen text:** Clear next steps for donors, families, and the chapter.

**Narration:**

> TABANG doesn't replace doctors, nurses, or a live blood service. It's a coordination prototype: requests with documents, checked donors, visible blood stock, and a chapter workflow that leaves a record.
>
> The goal is simple: make the next step clear for the person who needs blood, the person who can give it, and the chapter that keeps it safe.
>
> Signing in, account settings, the About page, and the Help guides are available in the app. This video focused on the blood workflow.
>
> Thank you.

## 4. Delivering the video and website in the Google Form

The form gives respondents **two separate links**. Do not combine them into one item, and do not expect respondents to search for the app on their own.

| Form item | Label respondents see | Link target |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Video orientation** | “Watch the TABANG orientation (7:30–8:00).” | View-only video link — YouTube **Unlisted** or a Google Drive **viewer** link |
| **2. TABANG website** | “Open the TABANG website.” | Web build URL, opened in a new tab |

### Before sending the form

- Make the video **view-only**. Share it as “Anyone with the link can view,” never “can edit,” and use the file link, not the folder link.
- Use the master video for the paper. Add the short cut as an optional second link only if the form has room, and label its runtime.
- Put each link in its own short-answer question or in the question description. Include the raw URL as plain text beside the clickable link so it still works if the form is printed or exported.
- Put the demo account respondents should use in the form itself, not only in the README. Use the donor account for the community flow; staff and admin accounts are for the panel or chapter reviewers.
- Open the form in a private window and on a phone. Click both links. The video must play without sign-in, and the website must load on a phone.
- Keep the form’s responses separate from the app. The website does not collect or receive the form answers.

### What to tell respondents

Put this short note above the links:

> Watch the video first. It shows how a family asks for blood, how donors help, and how chapter staff check requests. Then open the website to try the demo. The site uses made-up records, not real patient data. Sign in with a demo account shown on the site; do not enter real personal information.

### Access and fallback

- On YouTube, use **Unlisted**, not Private, so the link works without a channel invitation.
- If the site host shows a login wall or browser warning, fix it before sharing. Do not send respondents a workaround.
- Keep one backup copy of the video file and a plain-text list of both URLs with the form owner, in case a link expires.
- Do not make either link required for submission unless the instructor asks. A respondent who cannot open a link should still be able to finish the form.
- Demo passwords are acceptable here because the build is simulated. Do not reuse this pattern once the site holds real data.

## 6. Suggested description

> TABANG is a demo-data blood-donation coordination prototype for Digos City. This orientation shows how a family can ask for blood, how donors can help, and how chapter staff check requests, hold blood, and record donations. It also explains how the design handles fake online posts and paperwork done by hand, with RA 7719 (National Blood Services Act) and RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act) as its legal basis. No real patient data is used.  
> Demo accounts are listed in the app's README.  
> Built with Flutter; designed for Android and the web.

If this description is reused inside the Google Form, keep the video and website links as separate items. The description covers the video only; the website link needs its own label and instructions from section 4.
